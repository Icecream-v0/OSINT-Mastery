# The Glossary

Sixty terms you'll hear in this field, defined the way practitioners actually use
them. When someone says "the sock puppet got burned by a canvas fingerprint," this
page makes that sentence make sense.

## A–C

**ACH (Analysis of Competing Hypotheses)** — structured method where you list every
plausible explanation and test evidence against each one, instead of cherry-picking
support for your favorite. Fights confirmation bias by design.

**Admiralty code** — two-letter/number grading of sources: reliability A–F ×
credibility 1–6. "Source B2" means usually-reliable source, probably-true info.

**AIS (Automatic Identification System)** — ships' broadcast transponder signal.
Public, spoofable, and gaps in coverage near sensitive locations are themselves
informative.

**Aleph** — OCCRP's free research platform combining corporate registries, sanctions
lists, and leaked document databases. Financial investigation cheat mode.

**Archive.today** — snapshot service that captures pages Wayback struggles with,
including JavaScript-heavy content. archive.ph is the main mirror.

**BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)** — report structure where conclusions come first,
evidence after. Decision-makers read three lines; make those three lines count.

**Burned (an account/persona)** — a sock puppet whose connection to you or your other
personas has been discovered. Not fixable. Retire it.

**Canvas fingerprinting** — tracking technique identifying browsers by how they render
graphics. Why fresh browser profiles don't guarantee anonymity against serious targets.

**CDX API** — Wayback Machine's query interface listing every archived URL matching a
pattern. The difference between using archives and actually knowing how.

**Chain analysis** — tracing cryptocurrency flows between addresses, clustering those
controlled by one actor, and attributing deposits to exchanges.

**crt.sh** — certificate transparency search. Every SSL cert ever issued for a domain,
including internal subdomains nobody meant to expose.

## D–F

**Dork (Google dork)** — a search query using advanced operators to find exposed or
overlooked content. Named affectionately; feared by sysadmins.

**Doxxing** — publishing private identifying information to intimidate or endanger.
Not OSINT. The community's line in the sand.

**Epieos** — tool revealing Google/Microsoft account linkage from an email address,
plus registrations across services. Feels like cheating; isn't.

**EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format)** — metadata inside photos: GPS, device,
timestamps. Stripped by most platforms but alive in original files, email attachments,
and forum uploads.

**Finint (FININT)** — financial intelligence: registries, property records,
sanctions lists, crypto tracing.

**Footprinting** — mapping an organization's external surface: domains, employees,
tech stack, leaks. Phase one of both attacks and defenses.

**FOCO** — Facts, Observations, Conclusions, Opinions. Labeling your statements with
which category they belong to. Where reports earn trust.

## G–I

**GEOINT** — geospatial intelligence: photos, satellite imagery, maps, shadows,
mountains. Bellingcat's home turf.

**Geofencing** — collecting content by geographic area rather than keyword or account.

**GOSI** — GIAC Open Source Intelligence certification. Currently the credential HR
departments recognize most.

**Graph search** — Facebook's old natural-language search ("people who live in X and
work at Y"). Killed in 2019, mourned daily, partially replaced by x-ray searching.

**Holehe** — checks whether an email is registered on 120+ sites without triggering
notifications. Maps someone's account footprint fast.

**HUMINT boundary** — the line where open-source research becomes human-source
operation. Chatting with targets under false pretenses crosses it.

**ICD-203** — US intelligence directive standardizing probability language:
"highly likely" = 80–95%, and so on. Use the words precisely.

**Imagery intelligence** — see GEOINT. Also: satellite passes over an area are
publicly predictable, which matters for verification work.

**Intelligence cycle** — direction → collection → processing → analysis →
dissemination → feedback. The discipline separating analysts from scrapers.

**IPPI** — Internet Protocol Privacy Indicator? No — made-up example reminding you:
verify acronyms before repeating them. Half of intel jargon is unit-specific.

## J–O

**KartaView / Mapillary** — crowdsourced street-level imagery covering places Google's
cars never reached. Geolocation lifeline outside big cities.

**KYC (Know Your Customer)** — identity checks exchanges and banks must perform. The
reason crypto tracing stops at exchange doors without legal process.

**Link analysis** — drawing entities as nodes and relationships as edges, ideally with
each edge labeled by its supporting evidence. Unlabeled edges are decoration.

**Maltego** — the classic link-analysis platform with data transforms. Free Community
edition covers learning needs.

**Metadata** — data about data. Document authors, photo GPS, file creation paths.
Where files leak their life stories.

**MISP** — threat-intelligence sharing platform format. You'll meet it in corporate
roles.

**Nitter** — formerly beloved Twitter frontend viewer. Mostly dead since 2023 API
changes; listed so you stop wasting time on it.

**OPSEC (operations security)** — protecting your own identity, intent, and methods
during investigation. Chapter-length obsession for good reason.

**OSINT** — open source intelligence: finding, verifying, and packaging publicly
available information into decision-support. The craft this site teaches.

**Overpass Turbo** — query interface for OpenStreetMap data. "All water towers within
10km of these coordinates" — answered in seconds.

## P–S

**PACER** — US federal court records system. Ugly, mildly priced, indispensable.

**PEP (Politically Exposed Person)** — individual entrusted with prominent public
function, plus family/close associates. Standard due-diligence flag category.

**Photon** — crawler extracting intel (emails, social accounts, files) from websites.
Recon-ng's friendly cousin.

**PIR (Priority Intelligence Requirement)** — the specific question your collection
must answer. Written down before you start, or you'll drown in rabbit holes.

**Pushshift/Pullpush** — Reddit's historical archive APIs. Pullpush is the community
successor; essential for deleted-comment archaeology.

**Recon-ng** — modular web reconnaissance framework. Database-driven, module-based,
worth learning properly.

**Reverse image search** — querying by image instead of text. Yandex for faces,
TinEye for provenance, Lens for objects.

**Sandbox** — isolated environment for safely examining suspicious files/sites.
Snapshot your VM first.

**Sherlock** — CLI username enumeration across hundreds of platforms. Noisy, fast,
classic.

**Shodan** — search engine for internet-connected devices: cameras, databases,
industrial systems, indexed by banner. `ssl:` queries find servers by certificate.

**Sinker** — deliberately planted fake detail used to identify leakers. If a document
contains odd unique errors, consider that someone wanted them noticed.

**Sock puppet** — investigative alias account with consistent fictional identity,
aged history, and zero ties to your real self. Professional standard for interactions.

**SOCMINT** — social media intelligence. The people-watching branch.

**SpiderFoot** — automated OSINT collection framework sweeping 200+ sources. Good
first-pass sweep, noisy results.

**Steganography** — hiding data inside images/audio. Rare in practice, overrepresented
in CTFs.

**SunCalc** — shadow-position calculator. Turns "the shadow points northeast" into a
time, date, and hemisphere.

## T–Z

**Threat actor attribution** — claiming X conducted Y. Requires convergent evidence
across multiple independent pivots. One shared IP address is a lead, not a verdict.

**Tor (The Onion Router)** — anonymity network routing traffic through three relays;
.onion services live inside it. Access via Tor Browser only.

**Trace Labs** — CTF organization where teams OSINT-search for real missing persons.
Best practice venue with real impact.

**Verification** — confirming content is what it claims to be: original, correctly
dated, correctly located. Reverse search + geolocation + metadata + corroboration.

**Wayback Machine** — the Internet Archive's web snapshot service. Deleted pages go
there to be found again.

**Whaling** — targeting senior executives. Their public footprints are why OSINT
matters to your company's security team.

**X-ray searching** — using Google/Bing operators to extract content from behind
login walls via their indexes. LinkedIn OSINT's backbone.

**Zero-day** — vulnerability without a patch. Mentioned here because OSINT about a
company's tech stack (BuiltWith, job postings) feeds directly into such exploits —
which is exactly why defenders should run the same searches attackers do.

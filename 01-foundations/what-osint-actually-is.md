# What OSINT Actually Is

OSINT stands for Open Source Intelligence. Strip away the mystique and it's this:
answering questions using information that's already out there, then packaging the
answer so someone can act on it.

The key word isn't "open." It's "intelligence." Anyone can Google something. An OSINT
practitioner turns scattered public fragments into a defensible conclusion.

## Where it gets used

- **Journalism** — Bellingcat identified the Skripal poisoning suspects from travel
  databases and leaked records. All public sources.
- **Law enforcement** — missing persons cases, fraud rings, online predators.
- **Corporate security** — knowing what attackers know about your company before they
  use it against you.
- **Fraud and AML teams** — banks investigating who's really behind an account.
- **Penetration testing** — the recon phase. Most breaches start with public info.
- **Trust and safety** — platforms investigating networks of fake accounts.

## The flavors you'll hear about

- **SOCMINT** — social media intelligence
- **GEOINT** — geospatial (photos, satellite imagery, maps)
- **FININT** — financial (corporate records, crypto, sanctions lists)
- **TECHINT** — technical (domains, infrastructure, leaked credentials)

Most real investigations blend all four within the first hour.

## The mindset (this matters more than tools)

Three habits separate practitioners from people who watch YouTube videos about OSINT:

1. **Pivot thinking.** You never "find a person." You find a username, which leads to
   an email, which leads to a breach record, which leads to a phone number, which
   confirms the original username. Everything is a thread to pull.

2. **Document as you go.** Screenshot with timestamps and visible URLs. Save pages.
   Six weeks from now you'll need to prove where a claim came from, and the page will
   be deleted by then. It's always deleted by then.

3. **Distrust yourself.** That perfect match on the first search? It's usually wrong.
   Common names, reused usernames, coincidental photos — false positives are the
   default state of this work. Verification is the job.

## What OSINT is not

- It's not hacking. No exploiting, no password guessing, no bypassing logins.
- It's not doxxing. Publishing someone's home address to intimidate them is abuse,
  not intelligence.
- It's not magic. Half the craft is accepting "I couldn't determine X" as a valid
  finding.

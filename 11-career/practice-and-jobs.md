# Practice & Jobs

Skill in this field comes from reps on real problems. Here's where to practice
legally, and where the paid work actually lives.

## Practice venues

**Trace Labs** (tracelabs.org) — CTFs where teams OSINT-search for real missing
persons, submissions verified before forwarding to authorities. The best practice
format that exists: real stakes, real techniques, ethical frame. Free public events
run constantly; register early, they fill.

**GeoGuessr** — daily challenges build the geolocation instinct faster than anything.
Competitive modes exist if you like suffering.

**Quiztime** — professional geolocators post verification puzzles on X/Twitter.
Follow the regulars; attempt before reading replies.

**Bellingcat exercises** — published challenges from real investigations, plus their
excellent free guides. Their Discord/community discusses technique openly.

**r/OSINT** — case discussions, tool news, occasional collaborative mysteries.
Quality varies; the weekly threads are useful.

**OSINT Dojo** — free structured ranks (white → black belt) with practical
challenges per level. Good gap-filler between beginner resources and real work.

**HackTheBox Sherlocks** — defensive investigation scenarios blending OSINT with
forensics. Closer to blue-team work but builds rigor.

## Where the jobs are

| Sector | Roles | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Corporate threat intel** | Analyst, Intel Manager | Most common path; SOC-adjacent |
| **Fraud/AML (banks, fintech)** | Investigator | Stable, well-paid, regulatory-driven demand |
| **Trust & Safety (big tech)** | Investigator | Platform abuse, influence ops |
| **Journalism** | Verification/Investigative | Bellingcat model; portfolio-driven |
| **Insurance** | SIU Investigator | Underappreciated niche; steady |
| **Law enforcement/Gov** | Analyst | Requires citizenship/clearances usually |
| **Private investigation** | PI | License territory varies by jurisdiction |
| **Red team recon** | Offensive security | OSINT as phase one of pentesting |

## Getting noticed (the real game)

1. **Public writeups** — sanitized case studies showing your method. One good
   writeup of tracing a scam network beats ten certificates in hiring conversations.
2. **Tool contributions** — even documentation PRs to WhatsMyName/Sherlock get your
   name in the community.
3. **CTF rankings** — Trace Labs leaderboards are public and checked.
4. **Conference talks** — local BSides chapters welcome OSINT talks; recording goes
   on your profile forever.
5. **Newsletter presence** — responding to Quiztime puzzles publicly, being visibly
   correct, compounds over time.

## Salary reality

Varies wildly by region/sector: fraud analyst roles start modest; senior threat
intel and T&S investigator roles at major tech pay tech salaries; elite consulting
commands premium rates. The differentiator at every level is demonstrated judgment,
not tool count.

## The uncomfortable truth about entry

Nobody hires "OSINT person" fresh with zero portfolio. Everyone starts adjacent —
SOC analyst who does recon well, journalist who verifies well, fraud analyst who
finds people well — then converges. Pick your adjacent door and walk through it
with this folder's skills already loaded.

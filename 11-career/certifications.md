# Certifications — Getting the OSINT Title

Straight talk: OSINT has no licensing board. Nobody legally "is" or "isn't" an
OSINT professional. Certifications exist anyway, and they matter for one reason —
HR departments and contractors need paper. Here's the honest landscape.

## The certifications that carry weight

### GOSI — GIAC Open Source Intelligence (the gold standard)

- Backed by SANS via their **SEC497** course ("Open-Source Intelligence")
- The cert employers actually recognize in government/contractor circles
- Cost reality: SEC497 + GOSI attempt runs ~$8,000+ USD. Usually employer-funded.
- A standalone GOSI exam attempt (~$1,000ish) exists but is genuinely hard without
  the course structure
- Open-book, proctored, hands-on practical questions

If someone pays for it, take it. If you're self-funding, read the rest first.

### Michael Bazzell's training & book

- *"OSINT Techniques"* (currently 12th edition) — the practitioner's bible,
  ~$60. Working through every chapter IS a certification-grade education.
- His personal privacy books pair with it if OPSEC interests you
- No official cert attached; the community respect is the credential

### OSINT Combine

- Australian training company respected in government circles
- Courses run periodically; not cheap but well-regarded regionally

## Free/cheap credentials that actually signal skill

These aren't certificates — they're better, because they're verifiable:

1. **Trace Labs CTF participation** — public leaderboards. Finishing events puts
   your name on tracelabs.org permanently. Recruiters in this space check.
2. **OSINT Dojo ranks** — free structured challenges, white through black belt,
   verifiable badges.
3. **TryHackMe OSINT path** — beginner scaffolding with completion badges. Good
   first week; outgrow it fast.
4. **Public writeups** — the true currency. One thorough sanitized case study on a
   blog beats any badge in hiring conversations.

## The recommended path (costs almost nothing)

```
Weeks 1–4:   This folder + TryHackMe OSINT path
Months 2–3:  GeoGuessr daily + Bellingcat exercises + Quiztime attempts
Month 4:     First Trace Labs event (public ones welcome newcomers)
Month 5+:    Write up everything you learned, publish, repeat
Ongoing:     Sector035's "Week in OSINT" newsletter to stay current
When funded: GOSI for the HR checkbox
```

## What certs don't do

They don't teach judgment. They don't substitute for a portfolio. Nobody senior in
this field cares what certificate you hold — they care whether your writeups show
disciplined method, whether your Trace Labs submissions were verified, and whether
you can explain why you graded a source B3 instead of A2.

## Bottom line

Title = portfolio + community presence + (optionally) one recognized cert for the
paperwork people. Build in that order. The folder you're holding covers the first
part completely — now go do the exercises instead of just reading them.

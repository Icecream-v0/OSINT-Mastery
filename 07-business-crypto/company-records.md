# Company Records

Corporate registries are public by design — transparency laws mean most companies
must disclose who owns and runs them. Most people never look. You will.

## The registries that matter

**OpenCorporates** — 200+ million companies from registries worldwide, searchable in
one place. Directors, registered agents, filing histories. The aggregator start point.

**UK Companies House** — the world's most useful free registry: every UK company's
filings, accounts, officer lists, AND the **PSC register** (People with Significant
Control) naming actual beneficial owners. Journalists call it a cheat code for a
reason.

**US state Secretary of State sites** — Delaware (opaque ownership, millions of
shells), Wyoming, Nevada are the classic formation states; each has its own search.
No federal registry exists, which is itself the loophole.

**SEC EDGAR** — US public company filings: annual reports (10-K), executive
compensation, major shareholder disclosures (13D/13F), insider trading forms.
Full-text search finds names across decades of filings.

**EU BRIS** — European branch registers interconnection. Patchy but improving.

**OCCRP Aleph** — investigative consortium's database combining registries, sanctions
lists, leaked databases (Panama Papers et al.), and documents. Free for researchers;
arguably the single most powerful corporate research tool that exists.

**ICIJ Offshore Leaks** — searchable index of the Panama/Paradise/Pandora Papers.
If an entity appears here, the leaks contain its real paperwork.

## Sanctions and watchlists

- **OpenSanctions** — aggregates OFAC SDN, EU, UN lists plus PEPs (politically
  exposed persons) into one searchable database. Standard due-diligence tool.
- **OFAC Sanctions List Search** — the primary US list directly.

## Courts

- **PACER** — US federal cases (~$0.10/page, fee waivers under threshold)
- **CourtListener / RECAP** — free archive of documents others already paid for
- State courts vary wildly; most have at least name-searchable civil/criminal indices

## The techniques

1. **Officer cross-referencing** — same director across 15 companies = shell network.
   Map it visually (Maltego or draw.io).
2. **Registered agent addresses** — thousands of companies at one address = mass
   formation agent, not evidence of anything alone; but your target's LLC sharing
   an address with known bad actors is a lead.
3. **Filing timelines** — incorporation right before a contract award, dissolution
   right after a lawsuit: chronology is narrative.
4. **Follow the accountants** — the same audit firm/signatory recurring across
   entities links networks owners tried to keep separate.

## Glassdoor/Indeed side-channel

Employee reviews complain about specific clients, projects, and internal scandals
with dates. Unofficial, biased, occasionally brilliant.

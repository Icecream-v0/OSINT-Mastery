# Faces and Names

Two directions here: you have a name and want everything attached to it, or you have
a face and want the name. Both are core skills. Both come with ethical weight.

## Direction A: Name → Everything

Assume nothing about spelling first. Search variants: Jon/Jonathan/John, hyphenated
maiden names, transliterations for non-Latin names.

**People-search aggregators (US-heavy, free tiers):**
- FastPeopleSearch, TruePeopleSearch, That'sThem — these aggregate public records
  and are unsettlingly good: current/past addresses, relatives, associated phones
  and emails. Use for lawful investigations; these sites are exactly why OPSEC
  exists, by the way — go look yourself up and feel motivated.

**Public records:**
- Voter registration (public in most US states, searchable in many)
- Court records — PACER for US federal, state portals, CourtListener/RECAP (free
  archive of federal filings)
- Property records — county assessor/recorder sites (see 07-business-crypto/)
- Obituaries and wedding announcements — legacy.com, local papers. Obituaries are
  relationship maps: surviving family, cities lived in, maiden names.
- Professional licenses — state boards (nurses, realtors, contractors) are public
  and searchable.

**International note:** much of Europe restricts this class of data hard (GDPR).
UK electoral register is semi-public. Adjust expectations by jurisdiction.

## Direction B: Face → Name

Ranked by practical effectiveness:

1. **Yandex Images** — still the best free facial matching. Upload, crop tight to
   the face, scan results. Finds social profiles with startling regularity.
2. **PimEyes** — paid, powerful, controversial (it crawls faces aggressively).
   Legitimate investigators use it; so do stalkers, which is why regulators circle
   it. If you use it, have a documented lawful purpose.
3. **FaceCheck.ID** — focuses on offenders/news/media indexes.
4. **Google Lens** — weaker on pure face match but excellent when the face appears
   in indexed articles/pages.

Technique matters: crop to just the face, try multiple crops, try grayscale if
results are noisy. A hit usually means the same photo was uploaded somewhere —
follow it to the profile, then confirm with convergent evidence (usernames.md
rules apply).

## Deepfake reality check

Face swap tools are consumer-grade now. Before declaring "this video proves X,"
check: lighting consistency, blink rates, teeth, ear shape, edge flicker around
jaw/hairline, and whether any *other* evidence corroborates. FotoForensics and
InVID help but trained eyes beat tools here.

## The ethics weight

Face search is the technique most likely to be abused. Real-name-attribution of a
private person should require: lawful purpose, convergent multi-source confirmation,
and proportionate disclosure. Getting this wrong ruins innocent people — mixed-up
face matches have led to harassment campaigns against completely uninvolved folks.
Be the investigator who double-checks.
